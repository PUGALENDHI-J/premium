Before you go

Essential tips for travelling to Cambodia

Currency and payments

Cambodian riel (KHR); US dollars widely used. Carry some local currency for markets, small shops and tips, and ask your bank about international card charges before you travel.

Tipping

Not expected but appreciated; small US-dollar tips are common for guides and drivers.

Drinking water

Drink bottled or filtered water; it is cheap and easy to find.

Power plugs

Types A, C and G · 230 V. Indian two-pin plugs usually fit; carry an adapter for three-pin chargers.

Emergency numbers

117 (police), 119 (ambulance)

Language

Languages spoken: Khmer.

Getting around

Fly into Siem Reap's new airport or Phnom Penh; the two cities are about 5–6 hours apart by road or a short flight.

Planner's tip

Buy a multi-day Angkor pass; one day is not enough for the main temples.

Planner's tip

US dollars are accepted almost everywhere; carry small notes in good condition.

General guidance. Local rules and services change; we confirm details for your dates.

Everything you need to know about Cambodia

Cambodia is home to Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument in the world, built as a Hindu temple to Vishnu before becoming Buddhist. Siem Reap is the base for the Angkor temples, and Phnom Penh, the riverside capital, tells the harder story of the country's recent history.

We plan trips to 2 destinations in Cambodia: Siem Reap (Angkor) and Phnom Penh. Highlights include Angkor Wat and Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda. Local food worth trying: Fish amok, Lok lak, Khmer curry and Kuy teav. Our Cambodia itineraries run from 4 to 9 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

November to March. Cool and dry; the temples are hot by April.

Getting around

Fly into Siem Reap's new airport or Phnom Penh; the two cities are about 5–6 hours apart by road or a short flight.

Travel tips

Cambodia travel tips

Visa and entry

Entry rules for Indian passport holders change, including visa-free, visa-on-arrival and e-visa schemes, so confirm the current rules on the official government or embassy website for Cambodia before booking flights. Most countries require a passport valid for at least six months beyond your return date. We check the current requirements when we prepare your quote; we cannot guarantee visa approval.

Health and safety

Buy travel insurance that covers medical treatment and evacuation; healthcare abroad can be expensive. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. For current safety advice, check the travel advisories published by the Government of India and the embassy in Cambodia.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Cambodian riel (KHR); US dollars widely used. Not expected but appreciated; small US-dollar tips are common for guides and drivers.

Best time to visit

November to March. Cool and dry; the temples are hot by April.

Getting around

Fly into Siem Reap's new airport or Phnom Penh; the two cities are about 5–6 hours apart by road or a short flight.

Local etiquette

  • Dress modestly at temples, churches and mosques, and remove footwear where asked.
  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.

What to pack

Pack modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees for religious sites and comfortable walking shoes.

Phone and internet

Indian mobile operators sell international roaming packs, or you can buy a local SIM or eSIM on arrival with your passport. Check that your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM before you travel.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.
