Before you go

Essential tips for travelling to Jordan

Currency and payments

Jordanian dinar (JOD). Carry some local currency for markets, small shops and tips, and ask your bank about international card charges before you travel.

Tipping

Around 10% in restaurants; guides and drivers are customarily tipped.

Drinking water

Drink bottled or filtered water; it is cheap and easy to find.

Power plugs

Types B, C, D, F, G and J · 230 V. Indian two-pin plugs usually fit; carry an adapter for three-pin chargers.

Emergency numbers

911

Language

Languages spoken: Arabic.

Getting around

A private car and driver is the easiest way to travel the King's Highway and Desert Highway.

Planner's tip

The Jordan Pass covers the tourist visa fee and Petra entry for many visitors; we check eligibility for your trip.

Planner's tip

Spend at least two days at Petra; the Monastery climb alone takes half a day.

General guidance. Local rules and services change; we confirm details for your dates.

Everything you need to know about Jordan

Jordan holds Petra, the rose-red Nabataean city carved into canyon walls, the Martian landscapes of Wadi Rum, the salty waters of the Dead Sea and the Roman ruins of Jerash. It is compact enough to cover in a week.

We plan trips to 3 destinations in Jordan: Petra, Wadi Rum and Amman & the Dead Sea. Highlights include The Siq and the Treasury, Jeep desert tour and Amman Citadel. Local food worth trying: Mansaf, Maqluba, Falafel and hummus and Zarb (Bedouin underground barbecue). Our Jordan itineraries run from 5 to 9 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

March to May and September to November. Mild spring and autumn days; winter nights in the desert are cold.

Getting around

A private car and driver is the easiest way to travel the King's Highway and Desert Highway.

Travel tips

Jordan travel tips

Visa and entry

Entry rules for Indian passport holders change, including visa-free, visa-on-arrival and e-visa schemes, so confirm the current rules on the official government or embassy website for Jordan before booking flights. Most countries require a passport valid for at least six months beyond your return date. We check the current requirements when we prepare your quote; we cannot guarantee visa approval.

Health and safety

Buy travel insurance that covers medical treatment and evacuation; healthcare abroad can be expensive. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. For current safety advice, check the travel advisories published by the Government of India and the embassy in Jordan.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Jordanian dinar (JOD). Around 10% in restaurants; guides and drivers are customarily tipped.

Best time to visit

March to May and September to November. Mild spring and autumn days; winter nights in the desert are cold.

Getting around

A private car and driver is the easiest way to travel the King's Highway and Desert Highway.

Local etiquette

  • Dress modestly at temples, churches and mosques, and remove footwear where asked.
  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.
  • The Jordan Pass covers the tourist visa fee and Petra entry for many visitors; we check eligibility for your trip.

What to pack

Pack modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees for religious sites and comfortable walking shoes.

Phone and internet

Indian mobile operators sell international roaming packs, or you can buy a local SIM or eSIM on arrival with your passport. Check that your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM before you travel.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.
