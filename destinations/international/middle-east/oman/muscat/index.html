Everything you need to know about Muscat

Muscat is a low-rise, whitewashed capital between mountains and sea, with the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, the Mutrah Souq, the Royal Opera House and dolphin-watching trips along the coast.

Highlights include Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, Mutrah Souq and Corniche, Royal Opera House and Al Alam Palace. Local food worth trying: Shuwa, Omani halwa and Kahwa and dates. Plan 2–3 days here; it suits families, culture and nature lovers. Our Muscat itineraries run from 4 to 9 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

October to April. Comfortable; summer is very hot.

How to reach Muscat

  • By air: Muscat International Airport (MCT)
  • By rail: No railway
  • By road: Highways to Nizwa and Sur

Travel tips

Muscat travel tips

Visa and entry

Entry rules for Indian passport holders change, including visa-free, visa-on-arrival and e-visa schemes, so confirm the current rules on the official government or embassy website for Oman before booking flights. Most countries require a passport valid for at least six months beyond your return date. We check the current requirements when we prepare your quote; we cannot guarantee visa approval.

Health and safety

Buy travel insurance that covers medical treatment and evacuation; healthcare abroad can be expensive. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. For current safety advice, check the travel advisories published by the Government of India and the embassy in Oman.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Omani rial (OMR). Not obligatory; around 10% is appreciated.

Best time to visit

October to April. Comfortable; summer is very hot.

Getting around

A car with driver or a 4x4 self-drive is the best way to explore; distances between regions are long.

Local etiquette

  • Dress modestly at temples, churches and mosques, and remove footwear where asked.
  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.
  • Pack swimwear for the wadis but cover up in villages.
  • Friday mornings are quiet; plan souq visits for evenings.

What to pack

Pack sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat and swimwear, modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees for religious sites, comfortable walking shoes and a universal travel adapter.

Phone and internet

Indian mobile operators sell international roaming packs, or you can buy a local SIM or eSIM on arrival with your passport. Check that your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM before you travel.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.
