Before you go

Essential tips for travelling to Cusco & the Sacred Valley

Currency and payments

Peruvian sol (PEN). Carry some local currency for markets, small shops and tips, and ask your bank about international card charges before you travel.

Tipping

Around 10% in restaurants.

Drinking water

Drink bottled or filtered water; it is cheap and easy to find.

Power plugs

Types A and C · 220 V. Indian two-pin plugs usually fit; carry an adapter for three-pin chargers.

Emergency numbers

105 (police)

Language

Languages spoken: Spanish, Quechua.

Getting around

Fly Lima–Cusco; trains run from the Sacred Valley to Aguas Calientes below Machu Picchu.

Planner's tip

Machu Picchu tickets are timed and circuit-based; book months ahead.

Planner's tip

Spend two nights in the Sacred Valley before Cusco to adjust to the altitude.

Planner's tip

Sleep in the Sacred Valley first; it is lower than Cusco.

General guidance. Local rules and services change; we confirm details for your dates.

Everything you need to know about Cusco & the Sacred Valley

Cusco was the capital of the Inca Empire, and its colonial churches stand on Inca stone walls. The Sacred Valley below has terraces, markets and ruins at Pisac and Ollantaytambo.

Highlights include Plaza de Armas, Qorikancha, Sacsayhuamán and Pisac. Local food worth trying: Lomo saltado, Quinoa soup and Coca tea. Plan 3 days here; it suits history lovers, adventurers. Our Cusco & the Sacred Valley itineraries run from 5 to 7 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

May to September. Dry season.

How to reach Cusco & the Sacred Valley

  • By air: Alejandro Velasco Astete Airport (CUZ)
  • By rail: Trains from Poroy and Ollantaytambo
  • By road: Roads to the Sacred Valley

Travel tips

Cusco & the Sacred Valley travel tips

Visa and entry

Entry rules for Indian passport holders change, including visa-free, visa-on-arrival and e-visa schemes, so confirm the current rules on the official government or embassy website for Peru before booking flights. Most countries require a passport valid for at least six months beyond your return date. We check the current requirements when we prepare your quote; we cannot guarantee visa approval.

Health and safety

Buy travel insurance that covers medical treatment and evacuation; healthcare abroad can be expensive. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. Some places here are at high altitude: rest on arrival, drink plenty of water and ascend gradually. For current safety advice, check the travel advisories published by the Government of India and the embassy in Peru.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Peruvian sol (PEN). Around 10% in restaurants.

Best time to visit

May to September. Dry season.

Getting around

Fly Lima–Cusco; trains run from the Sacred Valley to Aguas Calientes below Machu Picchu.

Local etiquette

  • Dress modestly at temples, churches and mosques, and remove footwear where asked.
  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.

What to pack

Pack warm layers, a down jacket and gloves, modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees for religious sites and comfortable walking shoes.

Phone and internet

Indian mobile operators sell international roaming packs, or you can buy a local SIM or eSIM on arrival with your passport. Check that your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM before you travel.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.
