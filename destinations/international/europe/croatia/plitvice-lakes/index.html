Everything you need to know about Plitvice Lakes

Plitvice Lakes National Park is a chain of sixteen turquoise lakes linked by waterfalls and wooden boardwalks in the forested hills of central Croatia. Walking the boardwalks takes most of a day, and the colours are richest in late spring.

Highlights include Upper lakes, Lower lakes and Veliki Slap, Kozjak Lake boat and Panorama viewpoint. Local food worth trying: Spit-roasted lamb and Trout. Plan 1 days here; it suits nature lovers, photographers. Our Plitvice Lakes itineraries run from 5 to 5 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

May to June and September to October. Green and less crowded.

How to reach Plitvice Lakes

  • By air: Zagreb Airport (ZAG), about 2 hours
  • By rail: No railway
  • By road: Road between Zagreb and Zadar

Travel tips

Plitvice Lakes travel tips

Visa and entry

Entry rules for Indian passport holders change, including visa-free, visa-on-arrival and e-visa schemes, so confirm the current rules on the official government or embassy website for Croatia before booking flights. Most countries require a passport valid for at least six months beyond your return date. We check the current requirements when we prepare your quote; we cannot guarantee visa approval.

Health and safety

Buy travel insurance that covers medical treatment and evacuation; healthcare abroad can be expensive. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. For current safety advice, check the travel advisories published by the Government of India and the embassy in Croatia.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Euro (EUR). Around 10% for good service is appreciated.

Best time to visit

May to June and September to October. Green and less crowded.

Getting around

Buses and ferries link the coast; a car with driver is easiest for Plitvice.

Local etiquette

  • Dress modestly at temples, churches and mosques, and remove footwear where asked.
  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.

What to pack

Pack sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat and swimwear, modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees for religious sites and comfortable walking shoes.

Phone and internet

Indian mobile operators sell international roaming packs, or you can buy a local SIM or eSIM on arrival with your passport. Check that your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM before you travel.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.
