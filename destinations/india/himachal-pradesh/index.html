Before you go

Essential tips for travelling to Himachal Pradesh

Currency and payments

UPI and cards are widely accepted in cities and hotels; keep some cash for small shops, rural areas, hill towns and tips.

Tipping

Tipping is not compulsory; around 5–10% in restaurants and a small tip for drivers and guides is appreciated.

Drinking water

Drink bottled or filtered water; it is cheap and easy to find.

Emergency numbers

112 (all emergencies)

Language

Languages spoken: Hindi, Pahari.

Getting around

Shimla, Kullu (Bhuntar) and Dharamshala (Gaggal) have small airports with limited flights; most visitors fly to Chandigarh and drive. The Kalka–Shimla toy train is a UNESCO-listed ride.

Planner's tip

The Atal Tunnel has made Lahaul reachable year-round from Manali, but heavy snow still closes roads; keep a buffer day in winter.

Planner's tip

Spiti needs time to acclimatise; avoid flying straight into altitude.

General guidance. Local rules and services change; we confirm details for your dates.

Everything you need to know about Himachal Pradesh

Himachal Pradesh runs from colonial Shimla to the Tibetan Buddhist valleys of Spiti and Lahaul. In between are Manali's apple orchards and snow points, the Dalai Lama's home at McLeod Ganj, and the riverside cafés of the Parvati Valley.

We plan trips to 8 destinations in Himachal Pradesh: Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala & McLeod Ganj, Dalhousie & Khajjiar, Spiti Valley and Kasol & Parvati Valley and more. Highlights include The Ridge and Mall Road, Hadimba Temple, Tsuglagkhang Complex, Khajjiar Meadow and Key Monastery. Local food worth trying: Himachali dham, Siddu, Hot chai and momos on the Mall and Trout fish. Our Himachal Pradesh itineraries run from 3 to 10 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

March to June and October to December. Spring and autumn for clear views; December to February for snow in Shimla and Manali; June to September for Spiti and Lahaul.

Travel tips

Himachal Pradesh travel tips

Visa and entry

Indian citizens do not need a visa; carry a government photo ID such as Aadhaar or a passport for hotels and flights.

Health and safety

Travel insurance is a good idea, especially for adventure activities. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. Some places here are at high altitude: rest on arrival, drink plenty of water and ascend gradually.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Indian rupee (INR). Tipping is not compulsory; around 5–10% in restaurants and a small tip for drivers and guides is appreciated.

Best time to visit

March to June and October to December. Spring and autumn for clear views; December to February for snow in Shimla and Manali; June to September for Spiti and Lahaul.

Getting around

Shimla, Kullu (Bhuntar) and Dharamshala (Gaggal) have small airports with limited flights; most visitors fly to Chandigarh and drive. The Kalka–Shimla toy train is a UNESCO-listed ride.

Local etiquette

  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.

What to pack

Pack warm layers, a down jacket and gloves, a light jacket or sweater for cool evenings and comfortable walking shoes.

Phone and internet

Mobile data is good in towns and cities. Coverage can be patchy in remote, hill and forest areas, so download offline maps before you set off.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.
