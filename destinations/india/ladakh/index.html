Before you go

Essential tips for travelling to Ladakh

Currency and payments

UPI and cards are widely accepted in cities and hotels; keep some cash for small shops, rural areas, hill towns and tips.

Tipping

Tipping is not compulsory; around 5–10% in restaurants and a small tip for drivers and guides is appreciated.

Drinking water

Drink bottled or filtered water; it is cheap and easy to find.

Emergency numbers

112 (all emergencies)

Language

Languages spoken: Ladakhi, Hindi.

Getting around

Fly to Leh and rest before driving higher. Inner Line Permits are needed for Nubra, Pangong and other border areas; we arrange them.

Planner's tip

Spend at least 36–48 hours in Leh before going higher to acclimatise; avoid alcohol and exertion on day one.

Planner's tip

Carry cash; ATMs and card machines are scarce beyond Leh.

General guidance. Local rules and services change; we confirm details for your dates.

Everything you need to know about Ladakh

Ladakh is a high-altitude desert of monasteries, prayer flags and turquoise lakes on the far side of the Himalaya. Leh is the base for the Nubra Valley's sand dunes and Bactrian camels, Pangong Lake's changing blues and some of the highest motorable passes in the world.

We plan trips to 5 destinations in Ladakh: Leh, Nubra Valley, Pangong Lake, Markha Valley and Zanskar (Chadar trek). Highlights include Leh Palace, Khardung La, Pangong lakeshore, Kongmaru La and The Chadar. Local food worth trying: Thukpa, Skyu, Butter tea and Apricot jam. Our Ladakh itineraries run from 5 to 10 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

May to September. Roads and passes open and days are warm; nights stay cold. Winter is for experienced travellers only.

Getting around

Fly to Leh and rest before driving higher. Inner Line Permits are needed for Nubra, Pangong and other border areas; we arrange them.

Travel tips

Ladakh travel tips

Visa and entry

Indian citizens do not need a visa; carry a government photo ID such as Aadhaar or a passport for hotels and flights. Parts of Ladakh need an Inner Line Permit or Protected Area Permit for visitors; we arrange these as part of your trip.

Health and safety

Travel insurance is a good idea, especially for adventure activities. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. Some places here are at high altitude: rest on arrival, drink plenty of water and ascend gradually.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Indian rupee (INR). Tipping is not compulsory; around 5–10% in restaurants and a small tip for drivers and guides is appreciated.

Best time to visit

May to September. Roads and passes open and days are warm; nights stay cold. Winter is for experienced travellers only.

Getting around

Fly to Leh and rest before driving higher. Inner Line Permits are needed for Nubra, Pangong and other border areas; we arrange them.

Local etiquette

  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.

What to pack

Pack warm layers, a down jacket and gloves and comfortable walking shoes.

Phone and internet

Mobile data is good in towns and cities. Coverage can be patchy in remote, hill and forest areas, so download offline maps before you set off.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.

How many days do I need for Ladakh?

Is tap water safe to drink in Ladakh?

Do I need a permit to visit Ladakh?
