Before you go

Essential tips for travelling to Hemkund Sahib

Currency and payments

UPI and cards are widely accepted in cities and hotels; keep some cash for small shops, rural areas, hill towns and tips.

Tipping

Tipping is not compulsory; around 5–10% in restaurants and a small tip for drivers and guides is appreciated.

Drinking water

Drink bottled or filtered water; it is cheap and easy to find.

Emergency numbers

112 (all emergencies)

Language

Languages spoken: Hindi, Garhwali, Kumaoni.

Getting around

Dehradun (Jolly Grant) is the main airport; Pantnagar serves the Kumaon hills. Overnight trains from Delhi reach Haridwar, Dehradun and Kathgodam.

Planner's tip

Kedarnath and Badrinath need online registration with the state government before travel.

Planner's tip

Monsoon landslides (July–August) often block mountain roads; avoid tight schedules then.

Planner's tip

The climb from Ghangaria gains about 1,300 metres; start early, carry rain gear and turn back if you feel unwell.

Everything you need to know about Hemkund Sahib

Hemkund Sahib is a Sikh gurdwara beside a glacial lake at about 4,600 metres, one of the highest places of worship in the world, linked to Guru Gobind Singh. Pilgrims trek up from Govindghat via the base village of Ghangaria, which is also the starting point for the Valley of Flowers.

Highlights include Gurdwara Hemkund Sahib, Hemkund lake, Lakshman Temple and Valley of Flowers. Local food worth trying: Langar prasad, Kadha prashad and Hot parathas at Ghangaria. Plan 4–5 days here; it suits sikh pilgrims, trekkers. Our Hemkund Sahib itineraries run from 7 to 7 days and every one can be changed to suit you.

When to go

June to October. The gurdwara opens in late May or June and closes in October; flowers peak in July and August.

How to reach Hemkund Sahib

  • By air: Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun (DED), about 300 km
  • By rail: Rishikesh railway station
  • By road: About 10 hours by road from Rishikesh to Govindghat, usually split overnight

Travel tips

Hemkund Sahib travel tips

Visa and entry

Indian citizens do not need a visa; carry a government photo ID such as Aadhaar or a passport for hotels and flights.

Health and safety

Travel insurance is a good idea, especially for adventure activities. Carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a copy of the prescription. Some places here are at high altitude: rest on arrival, drink plenty of water and ascend gradually.

Currency and tipping

The currency is the Indian rupee (INR). Tipping is not compulsory; around 5–10% in restaurants and a small tip for drivers and guides is appreciated.

Best time to visit

June to October. The gurdwara opens in late May or June and closes in October; flowers peak in July and August.

Getting around

Dehradun (Jolly Grant) is the main airport; Pantnagar serves the Kumaon hills. Overnight trains from Delhi reach Haridwar, Dehradun and Kathgodam.

Local etiquette

  • Dress modestly at temples, churches and mosques, and remove footwear where asked.
  • Ask before photographing people, and follow photography rules at religious sites and museums.

What to pack

Pack warm layers, a down jacket and gloves, a light jacket or sweater for cool evenings, modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees for religious sites and comfortable walking shoes.

Phone and internet

Mobile data is good in towns and cities. Coverage can be patchy in remote, hill and forest areas, so download offline maps before you set off.

Budget

We don't publish fixed prices because the season, hotel category, flights and group size change the cost. Your written quote lists exactly what is included, so you can compare like for like.
