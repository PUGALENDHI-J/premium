Where to watch India's classical dance forms on holiday

Where travellers can watch Kathakali, Bharatanatyam, Odissi, Kathak and other Indian classical dances, including festival seasons and evening shows.

By the Nexara Holidays travel desk · Updated 24 October 2026 · 2 min read

India's classical dance forms combine music, storytelling and centuries-old technique. Watching a performance adds depth to any trip.

Kathakali, Kerala

Elaborate make-up, costumes and facial expressions tell stories from the epics. In Kochi, cultural centres stage short evening performances for visitors, often with a chance to watch the make-up being applied.

Mohiniyattam, Kerala

A graceful dance traditionally performed by women, sometimes included in cultural programmes in Kochi and Thiruvananthapuram.

Bharatanatyam, Tamil Nadu

One of India's oldest classical forms. Chennai's December music and dance season is the best time to see performances, at sabhas across the city. Kalakshetra in Chennai is a renowned institution.

Odissi, Odisha

A lyrical dance inspired by temple sculptures. The Konark Dance Festival in December is a highlight near Puri.

Kathak, North India

Spinning footwork and storytelling, associated with Lucknow and Jaipur. Look for performances in Delhi's cultural venues.

Kuchipudi, Andhra Pradesh

A dance-drama form named after a village in Andhra Pradesh.

Manipuri and Sattriya

Gentle, devotional forms from Manipur and Assam; Sattriya is linked to the monasteries of Majuli.

Folk dances

Rajasthan's folk performances at Bagore ki Haveli in Udaipur, and Kalbelia dancers in desert camps.

Dance festivals

  • Mamallapuram Dance Festival (December to January).
  • Khajuraho Dance Festival (February).
  • Konark Dance Festival (December).

See India's festival calendar for travellers: when to plan which trip and Cultural holidays.

Tips for first-time viewers

Read the story beforehand or ask for a programme note; many performances are based on episodes from the epics. Arrive early for a good seat, switch phones to silent and ask before taking photographs.
