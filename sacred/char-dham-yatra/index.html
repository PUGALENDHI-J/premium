Kedarnath Temple

The Himalayan yatras are the most demanding pilgrimages in India, and the most rewarding. The four dhams of Uttarakhand open on Akshaya Tritiya in April or May and close around Diwali, Vaishno Devi is open all year, and the Amarnath cave is open only for a few weeks in July and August.

We handle the compulsory registrations, book helicopter seats to Kedarnath where available, and plan overnight stops so that no day has too much driving on mountain roads. Ponies, palkis and porters can be arranged at the trailheads.

How we plan

Planned with reverence

Darshan and prayer timings

We plan each day around opening hours, aarti, Mass or prayer times, and book darshan, puja or VIP entry where the temple allows it.

Paced for elders

Fewer hotel changes, ground-floor or lift rooms on request, wheelchairs, ponies, palkis or helicopters where available.

Food that respects your faith

Pure vegetarian, Jain, satvik or halal meals arranged where possible, and we tell you honestly where options are limited.

Registrations and permits

Char Dham and Amarnath registration, Inner Line and Tibet permits, and medical certificates where they are required.

FAQs

Char Dham, Amarnath & Himalayan Yatras: common questions

When is the best time for the Char Dham Yatra?
May to June and September to October. The shrines open in late April or May and close around Diwali; July and August bring monsoon landslides on the mountain roads.
How fit do I need to be?
Kedarnath is a 16 km trek each way and Yamunotri 6 km; ponies, palkis and helicopters reduce the walking. Everyone should have a medical check-up before travelling above 3,000 metres.
How do I register for the Amarnath Yatra?
Registration opens in spring through the Shri Amarnathji Shrine Board and its designated banks, and needs a Compulsory Health Certificate from an authorised doctor. Pilgrims must be aged 13 to 70. We guide you through the forms, then collect your RFID card and book helicopter seats where you want them.
Pahalgam or Baltal: which Amarnath route is better?
The Pahalgam route is about 36 km and takes three days up, through Sheshnag and Panchtarni, with a gentler climb. The Baltal route is about 14 km and can be done up and back in a day or two, but it is much steeper. Helicopters fly to Panchtarni from both sides.
