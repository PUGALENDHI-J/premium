Adventure holidays

Adventure travel ranges from a gentle snorkel to a high-altitude trek. We match the trip to your fitness and experience, and we only use licensed, insured operators for activities.

Popular adventures

  • White-water rafting on the Ganga at Rishikesh (September to June).
  • Scuba diving for beginners on Havelock Island (Swaraj Dweep) in the Andamans, or certified dives in El Nido (Palawan) and off Cairns & the Great Barrier Reef.
  • High-altitude road trips in Leh and Spiti Valley (summer only).
  • Paragliding at Manali's Solang Valley, Pokhara and Bir.
  • Bungee and jet boats in Queenstown, the world's adventure capital.
  • Desert camps in Jaisalmer, Wadi Rum and Merzouga (Sahara).
  • Tamil Nadu's hills: forest treks around Kodaikanal and Valparai in the Western Ghats.

High-altitude treks and 5,000-metre camps

From Ladakh's passes to Everest Base Camp, these treks climb high. Heights are approximate.

TrekHighest pointSeasonDays on trail
Everest Base Camp (Nepal)Kala Patthar, about 5,545 m; Base Camp about 5,364 mMarch–May, October–November12
Mount Kilimanjaro (Tanzania)Uhuru Peak, about 5,895 mJanuary–March, June–October7
Markha Valley (Ladakh)Kongmaru La, about 5,200 mJune–September6
Ladakh high passes by roadKhardung La, above 5,000 m; Pangong camps about 4,250 mJune–September7
Annapurna Base Camp (Nepal)About 4,130 mMarch–May, October–November8
Hampta Pass (from Manali)About 4,270 mJune–September5
Kedarkantha (Uttarakhand)About 3,800 mDecember–April5
Zanskar (Chadar trek): the Chadar frozen riverAbout 3,400 m, nights far below freezingJanuary–February6

Above about 3,500 metres the air holds much less oxygen. Every high trek we plan starts with acclimatisation days, climbs gradually and uses licensed guides, and we ask for a medical check-up before you book. Altitude sickness can affect anyone, however fit: the rule on the mountain is to go down if symptoms get worse.

Safety first

Altitude, weather and operator standards matter. We build in acclimatisation days for Ladakh and Spiti, recommend travel insurance that covers the activities you plan, and tell you honestly when a season is not right for an activity.

FAQs

Adventure holidays FAQs

Do I need to be very fit?
Most adventure activities we plan suit people of average fitness. We flag strenuous ones, like long treks, in advance.
Can a beginner trek to Everest Base Camp?
Yes, if you are healthy and prepare. No climbing skills are needed, but you walk five to seven hours a day for about twelve days at altitude. Train with long walks and stair climbs for two to three months, and consider a shorter trek such as Kedarkantha or Annapurna Base Camp first.
Do you take care of permits?
Yes. We arrange the permits each trek needs, such as Inner Line and protected-area permits in Ladakh, Sagarmatha National Park entry for Everest and Kilimanjaro park fees, and book licensed local guides.
Is travel insurance required?
We strongly recommend insurance that specifically covers the adventure activities in your plan.
